# Electric Motors: Condition Monitoring and Care

Applies to all low-voltage induction motors on site rated up to 110 kW.

## Bearing Greasing

Motors with grease nipples must be regreased on the interval shown on the motor nameplate. When the nameplate gives no interval, use 4,000 running hours for 2-pole motors and 8,000 running hours for 4-pole motors. Two-pole motors run at roughly twice the speed and their bearings need grease more often. Use the grease type stated on the nameplate and never mix grease with a different thickener, such as lithium and polyurea, because mixed greases can soften and leak out of the bearing. Open the grease drain plug before greasing and run the motor for 30 minutes before closing it.

## Vibration Monitoring

Measure overall vibration velocity on each bearing housing every month with the portable analyser. Take readings in the horizontal, vertical and axial directions. For motors between 15 kW and 300 kW on rigid foundations, the site limits are: below 2.8 mm/s is good, 2.8 to 4.5 mm/s is acceptable, 4.5 to 7.1 mm/s requires planning a repair, and above 7.1 mm/s requires stopping the motor as soon as production allows. A steady rise of more than 25 percent between two monthly readings must be reported even if the value is still in the acceptable band.

High vibration at running speed often means unbalance or misalignment. High axial vibration points to misalignment in particular. Vibration at bearing defect frequencies, which shows as a rising noise floor in the spectrum, means the bearing is failing.

## Insulation Resistance Testing

Test the stator winding insulation with a 500 V or 1,000 V insulation tester every year and after any motor has been wet or stored for more than three months. Isolate and lock out the motor, disconnect the supply cables at the terminal box, and test each phase to earth for one minute. The minimum acceptable insulation resistance is 1 megohm per kilovolt of rated voltage plus 1 megohm, with an absolute minimum of 5 megohms for site motors. Record the readings with the winding temperature, because insulation resistance falls as temperature rises. A reading below the minimum means the winding must be dried out or rewound before it is energised.

## Overheating

A motor frame that is too hot to touch for more than a few seconds is above 60 degrees Celsius and needs investigation. Common causes of overheating are a blocked cooling fan cowl, dust on the cooling fins, low supply voltage, phase imbalance, and overloading. Measure the current in all three phases. Current imbalance of more than 10 percent between phases causes extra heating and usually comes from a loose connection or a supply fault. Clean the fan cowl and fins with low-pressure air and confirm that the motor current does not exceed the full-load current on the nameplate.

## Starting Frequency

Large motors must not be started too often, because the starting current heats the winding much more than normal running. Unless the motor data sheet allows more, limit motors above 30 kW to three starts per hour, evenly spaced.
