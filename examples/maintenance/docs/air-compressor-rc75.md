# RC-75 Rotary Screw Compressor: Maintenance Manual

Applies to the two RC-75 oil-injected rotary screw compressors in the compressed air room. They supply instrument and blow-moulding air for the bottling lines.

## Specifications

The RC-75 delivers 12.5 cubic metres per minute of free air at a working pressure of 7.5 bar. It is driven by a 75 kW motor. Compression heat is removed by an oil cooler and an aftercooler, both air-cooled. Normal discharge temperature at the airend is between 75 and 95 degrees Celsius. The controller trips the compressor at 110 degrees Celsius. The oil sump holds 38 litres of synthetic compressor oil.

## Daily Checks

Check the oil level in the sight glass with the compressor stopped for at least five minutes; the level must sit between the two marks. Record the working pressure, discharge temperature and running hours from the controller display. Confirm that the automatic condensate drains on the aftercooler and the receiver are cycling by pressing the test button and listening for a short blast of air and water. Look at the differential pressure indicator on the air intake filter; a red indicator means the filter must be changed.

## Condensate Management

Compressing humid air produces a large amount of water. A single RC-75 on a warm, humid day can produce more than 60 litres of condensate per shift. All condensate contains oil and must pass through the oil-water separator before it goes to the drain. Never bypass the separator. Empty and replace the separator's carbon cartridge every 4,000 hours or when the outlet water looks cloudy.

## Oil and Filter Service

Change the oil, oil filter and air-oil separator element every 8,000 running hours, or once a year if the compressor runs fewer hours. Drain the oil while it is warm so that contaminants stay suspended. Fill with fresh oil to the upper mark, run the compressor for ten minutes, stop it, wait five minutes and top up to the upper mark again. Mixing oil brands is not allowed, because additive packages can react and form varnish on the rotors.

Change the air intake filter every 2,000 hours or sooner if the differential indicator turns red. A blocked intake filter reduces output and raises the discharge temperature.

## Troubleshooting

High discharge temperature is the most common alarm. Check, in order: the oil level, the cooler fins for dust and fluff, the room ventilation, and the thermostatic valve. Dirty cooler fins can raise discharge temperature by 10 to 15 degrees. Clean them with low-pressure air blown from the fan side outward. If the thermostatic valve sticks closed, oil bypasses the cooler and the compressor trips at 110 degrees Celsius.

Oil carry-over into the air lines shows up as oily condensate at the drains or an oily film on the blow-moulding valves. The usual causes are an overfilled sump, a failed separator element, or a blocked scavenge line. Never top up above the upper mark on the sight glass.

Safety valve lifting means the pressure has gone above its setting of 8.5 bar. Check the pressure switch and the controller set points before resetting. Never adjust or plug a safety valve.

Frequent load and unload cycling wastes energy and wears the inlet valve. It usually means the receiver is too small for the demand pattern or the pressure band is set too narrow. The recommended band is 0.5 bar between load and unload pressure.
