# HP-30 Hydraulic Press: Maintenance Manual

Applies to the HP-30 30-tonne hydraulic press in the tool room, used for bearing and bush pressing.

## Specifications

The HP-30 has a 7.5 kW power unit with a gear pump, a 100-litre reservoir and a single-acting cylinder. The pressure relief valve is set to 210 bar. The system uses ISO VG 46 anti-wear hydraulic oil. Normal oil temperature in service is 40 to 55 degrees Celsius, and the press must be stopped if the oil reaches 65 degrees Celsius. A gas-charged accumulator smooths pump pulsation and is pre-charged with nitrogen to 90 bar.

## Daily Checks

Check the reservoir level in the sight glass with the cylinder fully retracted. Inspect hoses and fittings for leaks, chafing and bulging. Check the return filter indicator; when it shows red, the filter element must be changed. Test the two-hand control and the guard interlock before the first job of the day.

## Hydraulic Fluid Cleanliness

Contamination causes most hydraulic failures. The target cleanliness for the HP-30 is ISO 4406 code 18/16/13 or better. Take an oil sample every six months from the sampling point on the pressure line while the system is running at operating temperature. Water content must stay below 0.05 percent; milky oil indicates water ingress and must be changed. Always fill the reservoir through the filler-breather using a filter cart with a 10 micron element, never by pouring oil in directly through the open filler.

## Return Filter Change

Change the return filter element every 1,000 hours or when the indicator shows red. Stop the pump, retract the cylinder, and release pressure by operating the control valve in both directions with the pump off. Unscrew the filter bowl slowly, remove the element, and check it for metal particles, which point to a pump or cylinder that is wearing. Fit the new element with a lightly oiled seal, refit the bowl, and run the pump to check for leaks.

## Relief Valve Setting

The relief valve protects the cylinder, hoses and frame. Only an authorised technician may adjust it. Fit a calibrated gauge at the test point, run the cylinder to the end of its stroke, and read the pressure at which the relief valve opens. It must open at 210 bar plus or minus 5 bar. Adjust with the locknut released, then tighten the locknut and fit a tamper seal.

## Accumulator Safety

An accumulator stores energy even when the pump is off. Before any work on the hydraulic circuit, open the accumulator dump valve and confirm on the gauge that the pressure has fallen to zero. Check the nitrogen pre-charge every six months with the oil side fully discharged; it should be 90 bar. Only use dry nitrogen to charge an accumulator. Never use oxygen or compressed air, because they can cause an explosion when mixed with oil under pressure.

## Troubleshooting

Slow cylinder movement usually comes from low oil level, a worn pump, or a relief valve that is leaking back to tank. Check the level first, then the relief valve setting.

Overheating above 65 degrees Celsius is often caused by the relief valve opening during long holds, a clogged cooler, or using an oil grade that is too thin.

External leaks must be repaired, not just wiped up. Never feel for a hydraulic leak with your hand, because a pinhole jet at high pressure can inject oil through the skin. Use a piece of card to find the leak instead. An oil injection injury is a medical emergency even if it looks small.
