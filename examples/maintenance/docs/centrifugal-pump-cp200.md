# CP-200 Centrifugal Pump: Maintenance Manual

Northfield Bottling Plant, utilities section. Applies to the CP-200 end-suction pumps that move process water from the treatment tanks to the rinse lines.

## Specifications

The CP-200 is a single-stage end-suction centrifugal pump driven by a 15 kW, 2-pole electric motor at 2,950 rpm. Rated duty is 60 cubic metres per hour at 32 metres of head. The shaft is sealed by a cartridge mechanical seal with silicon carbide faces. The bearing frame holds two deep-groove ball bearings lubricated with lithium-complex grease. Maximum allowed bearing housing temperature is 80 degrees Celsius. The minimum net positive suction head required (NPSHr) at rated flow is 3.5 metres.

## Daily Checks

Walk the pump once per shift and record discharge pressure, suction pressure and motor current on the round sheet. Normal discharge pressure at rated flow is between 3.0 and 3.3 bar. Listen for gravel-like noise at the suction side, which usually indicates cavitation. Check the seal area for drips; a healthy cartridge seal should show no visible leakage. Touch-check the bearing housing with an infrared thermometer and log the reading.

## Starting the Pump

Before starting, make sure the suction valve is fully open and the pump casing is flooded. Never run the CP-200 dry, because the mechanical seal faces overheat and crack within seconds without liquid. Open the discharge valve about one quarter, start the motor, and then open the discharge valve slowly to the operating position while watching the pressure gauge. If discharge pressure does not build within 30 seconds, stop the pump and check for an air lock or a closed suction valve.

## Mechanical Seal Replacement

Replace the cartridge seal when leakage exceeds 10 drops per minute or when the seal faces are chipped. First isolate the pump using the lockout procedure, close both suction and discharge valves, and drain the casing through the drain plug. Remove the coupling guard and the spacer coupling. Loosen the seal gland nuts, then slide the cartridge seal off the shaft after setting the setting clips. Clean the shaft sleeve and check it for scoring; replace the sleeve if the scoring is deeper than 0.1 mm. Fit the new cartridge, tighten the gland nuts evenly in a cross pattern to 25 Nm, and remove the setting clips before rotating the shaft by hand. Refill and vent the casing before restarting.

## Bearing Lubrication

Grease each bearing every 2,000 running hours with 15 grams of lithium-complex grease. Remove the relief plug before greasing so that old grease can escape, run the pump for 20 minutes, and then refit the plug. Over-greasing is a common cause of high bearing temperature, because the bearing churns the excess grease and generates heat.

## Troubleshooting

Cavitation sounds like gravel passing through the pump and causes vibration and pitting of the impeller. It happens when the available suction head falls below the NPSHr of 3.5 metres. Common causes are a clogged suction strainer, a partly closed suction valve, or a low level in the supply tank. Clean the strainer first, then confirm the tank level is above the minimum mark.

Low discharge pressure with normal motor current usually points to a worn impeller or excessive wear ring clearance. The wear ring clearance should not exceed 0.6 mm on the diameter; above that, recirculation inside the pump reduces head.

High motor current with high flow means the pump is running too far out on its curve. Throttle the discharge valve until the flow returns to the rated 60 cubic metres per hour.

High bearing temperature above 80 degrees Celsius requires the pump to be stopped. Check for over-greasing, misalignment of the coupling, and damaged bearings. Shaft alignment must be within 0.05 mm offset after any coupling work.
