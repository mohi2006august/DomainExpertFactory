# Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) Procedure

Site procedure for isolating hazardous energy before maintenance, cleaning or clearing a jam on any machine.

## Scope

This procedure applies whenever a person must put any part of their body into a danger zone of a machine, remove a guard, or bypass a safety device. Energy sources include electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, thermal and gravity. Every machine has a machine-specific isolation sheet that lists all of its isolation points.

## The Six Steps

1. Prepare: read the isolation sheet and identify every energy source and isolation point.
2. Notify: tell the operators and the shift lead that the machine will be shut down and locked out.
3. Shut down: stop the machine using its normal stop controls.
4. Isolate: operate every isolation device listed on the sheet, such as the main disconnect, air valves and hydraulic valves.
5. Lock and tag: fit your personal padlock and a signed danger tag to every isolation point.
6. Verify: confirm zero energy before starting work.

## Verifying Zero Energy

Verification is the step people skip, and it is the step that saves lives. After isolating, try to start the machine with its normal start button and confirm nothing moves, then return the control to off. Use a voltage tester that you have proved on a known live source before and after testing the circuit. Bleed off compressed air and hydraulic pressure and check that gauges read zero. Block or lower any part that could fall under gravity, and let hot surfaces cool.

## Stored Energy

Isolation does not remove stored energy. Springs, raised loads, flywheels that are still turning, capacitors in drives, accumulators and trapped air in cylinders can all move a machine after the supply is off. Discharge or block every stored energy source listed on the isolation sheet before starting work.

## Personal Locks

Each person working on the machine fits their own padlock. Locks and keys are personal and must never be lent. Only the person who fitted a lock may remove it. If the person has left site with the lock still fitted, the lock may only be removed by the shift lead after trying to contact the person, confirming they are not on site, and checking the machine is safe. The removal must be recorded and the person told before their next shift.

## Group Lockout

For large jobs with many workers, the supervisor locks the isolation points and places the keys in a lockbox. Every worker then fits a personal lock to the lockbox. The supervisor's keys cannot be reached until the last worker has removed their lock.

## Shift Change

Locks must never be left without an owner. At a shift change, the incoming worker fits their lock before the outgoing worker removes theirs, so that the machine is never unprotected.

## Returning to Service

Remove tools and materials, refit all guards, and check that everyone is clear of the machine. Each person removes their own lock and tag. Notify the operators, then restore energy and test the machine.
